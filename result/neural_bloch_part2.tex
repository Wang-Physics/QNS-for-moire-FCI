\part{Neural many-body Bloch wavefunctions}

\section{Motivation and scope}

The second calculation tests the neural many-body Bloch construction of
Ref.~\cite{luo} directly in the continuum model benchmarked in Part~I. The
purpose is not to fit coefficients in the one-band ED Hilbert space. Instead,
a graph network dresses continuum Bloch orbitals in coordinate space and the
resulting generalized Slater determinant is optimized against the complete
interacting continuum Hamiltonian. This can generate remote-band content even
though the fixed one-body input is the mean-field first band.

This revision follows Appendix~B, Eqs.~(16)--(23), of Ref.~\cite{luo}.
In particular, the orbital transformation is the direct complex output
\begin{equation}
 J_{\bm k i}(X)=\operatorname{MLP}([\bm k,V_i^t])\in\mathbb C,
 \qquad Q_{\bm k i}=J_{\bm k i}\varphi_{\bm k i}.
 \label{eq:scope-direct-j}
\end{equation}
There is no constant-one skip, residual parameterization, or factor $0.2$;
the implemented transformation is the direct $J_{\bm k i}$ above.
The current source additionally implements three audit corrections: full-$M$
uses an unbiased complex random initialization; the node aggregation is the
literal $\sum_jm_{ij}$ in Eq.~(19), including $j=i$; and the random auxiliary
features $h_i^0,h_{ij}^0$ are explicitly retained in the trainable parameter
tree. Version 4 retains one dense complex matrix $M$ but projects the complete
generalized determinant onto total momentum $\Gamma$ by an exact finite
translation average. By Cauchy--Binet this keeps all and only the
$\Gamma$-sector minors, with mutually constrained coefficients
$\det M_{:,I}$, without enumerating momentum combinations or introducing a
second determinant-mixing tensor. Earlier one-hot-$M$, no-self-message,
single-minor and v3 $M_S$ checkpoints are excluded from the v4 comparison.

Both v4 $\Gamma$-projected runs and all six v4 outer-$C_3$ branches use the
same 120-update protocol described below. The
internal-equivariance construction investigated in v2 is omitted from the
present results because its threefold evaluation was expensive and did not
improve the controlled energy comparison. Quoted training
energies use the final ten persistent-chain measurements; the corresponding
RMS is a stability diagnostic rather than an independent error bar.


\section{Configuration, fixed Bloch basis, and conventions}

A walker is a first-quantized configuration
\begin{equation}
 X=\{(\bm r_i,l_i)\}_{i=1}^{N_e},\qquad
 \bm r_i\in[0,1)^2,\quad l_i^{\rm code}\in\{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
The fractional coordinate is converted to a Cartesian coordinate on the
$3\times3$ torus. The layer input used by the network is
\begin{equation}
 s_i=2l_i^{\rm code}-1\in\{-1,+1\},
\end{equation}
which is the $l_i=\pm1$ convention of Ref.~\cite{luo}. Particle labels are
only row labels of the determinant and graph; no ordered occupation string is
used.

The nine mesh momenta and plane-wave orbitals are
\begin{equation}
 \bm k_{m_1m_2}=\frac{m_1}{3}\bm b_1+
 \frac{m_2}{3}\bm b_2,\qquad m_1,m_2=0,1,2,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \phi^0_{\bm k}(\bm r,l)=
 \sum_{\bm G}u^l_{1\bm k\bm G}
 e^{i(\bm k+\bm G)\cdot\bm r}.
 \label{eq:strictbare}
\end{equation}
The reciprocal set contains the same 61 $\bm G$ vectors per layer used for
the single-particle bands in Fig.~\ref{fig:bands}. The coefficients
$u^l_{1\bm k\bm G}$ are obtained once by diagonalizing the continuum
mean-field Hamiltonian. In JAX they are constants, and in PyTorch they are
registered buffers. They are absent from the trainable parameter tree. Thus
every run uses band 1 as its mean-field orbital source; neither ED amplitudes
nor trainable plane-wave coefficients are used.

\section{Appendix-B graph network}

\subsection{Initial physical and auxiliary features}

The three reciprocal harmonics used at each vertex are
$\bm g_a\in\{\bm b_1,\bm b_2,\bm b_1+\bm b_2\}$. The immutable node
feature is
\begin{equation}
 v_i^0=\left[
 \sin(\bm g_a\!\cdot\!\bm r_i),
 \cos(\bm g_a\!\cdot\!\bm r_i),s_i
 \right]\in\mathbb R^7 .
 \label{eq:nodefeature}
\end{equation}
For every ordered pair $(i,j)$, the immutable edge feature is
\begin{equation}
 e_{ij}^0=\left[
 \sin\!\left(\bm k\!\cdot\!(\bm r_i-\bm r_j)\right),
 \cos\!\left(\bm k\!\cdot\!(\bm r_i-\bm r_j)\right),s_i
 \right]\in\mathbb R^{19},
 \label{eq:edgefeature}
\end{equation}
where all nine mesh momenta are included. In particular, the edge layer
channel is the source layer $s_i$, exactly as in Eq.~(16) of
Ref.~\cite{luo}; no additional layer channels are appended.

The complete initial features are
\begin{equation}
 V_i^0=(v_i^0,h_i^0)\in\mathbb R^{32},\qquad
 E_{ij}^0=(e_{ij}^0,h_{ij}^0)\in\mathbb R^{32}.
\end{equation}
The code therefore uses 25 auxiliary node components and 13 auxiliary edge
components. Appendix~B explicitly calls $h_i^0$ and $h_{ij}^0$ ``randomly
initialized parameters.'' They are therefore leaves of the trainable JAX
pytree (and \texttt{nn.Parameter} objects in PyTorch), so natural-gradient
updates act on them together with the other network parameters. The present
code uses one random vector shared over particle labels and one shared over
ordered pairs; sharing preserves permutation equivariance. Each component is
initialized from $\mathcal N(0,1/32)$. The paper does not specify this
distribution or sharing rule, so both remain explicit local conventions.
There is no learned input embedding before Eq.~\eqref{eq:message}.

\subsection{Two message-passing iterations}

For iteration $a=1,2$,
\begin{align}
 Q_{ij}^{a}&=W_Q^{a}E_{ij}^{a-1},\\
 K_{ij}^{a}&=W_K^{a}E_{ij}^{a-1},\\
 m_{ij}^{a}
 &=F^{a}\!\left(\sum_n
 Q_{in}^{a}\otimes K_{nj}^{a}\right)
 \odot G^{a}(E_{ij}^{a-1}),                             \label{eq:message}\\
 h_i^{a}
 &=h_i^{a-1}+U^{a}\!\left(
 [\sum_jm_{ij}^{a},V_i^{a-1}]\right),\\
 h_{ij}^{a}
 &=h_{ij}^{a-1}+H^{a}\!\left(
 [m_{ij}^{a},E_{ij}^{a-1}]\right),\\
 V_i^{a}&=(v_i^0,h_i^{a}),\\
 E_{ij}^{a}&=(e_{ij}^0,h_{ij}^{a}).
 \label{eq:graphupdate}
\end{align}
The outer product in Eq.~\eqref{eq:message} is flattened before $F^a$.
$F^a,G^a,H^a,U^a$ are one-layer real affine maps followed by exact GELU.
The two iterations have independent parameters. No division by $N_e$, no
neighbor-count normalization, and no LayerNorm is applied. Self edges remain
available to both the edge update and node aggregation. This is the literal
$\sum_j$ printed in Luo--Fu Eq.~(19), rather than the ``all other particles''
neighborhood convention of Ref.~\cite{pescia}. The literal physical features
$v_i^0,e_{ij}^0$ are reinserted after every iteration.

\section{Backflow, orbital transformation, and determinant}

The complex backflow matrix is implemented as a real, bias-free
$32\to4$ linear map. Its first and last two outputs are the real and
imaginary parts of a two-component displacement,
\begin{equation}
 \delta\bm r_i(X)=W V_i^2,\qquad
 \widetilde{\bm r}_i=\bm r_i+\delta\bm r_i .
 \label{eq:strictbackflow}
\end{equation}
There is no $\tanh$, clipping, or external backflow scale. The fixed
mean-field coefficients are evaluated only at this transformed coordinate,
\begin{equation}
 \varphi_{\bm k i}(X)=
 \sum_{\bm G}u^{l_i}_{1\bm k\bm G}
 \exp\!\left[i(\bm k+\bm G)\cdot
 (\bm r_i+\delta\bm r_i(X))\right].
 \label{eq:strictphi}
\end{equation}
Consequently, backflow does not update $u^l_{1\bm k\bm G}$. The factors
$\exp[i(\bm k+\bm G)\cdot\delta\bm r_i]$ are generated only by changing the
coordinate argument.

The neural orbital transformation uses the Cartesian two-component mesh
momentum and the final node feature,
\begin{equation}
 [V_i^2,k_x,k_y]\in\mathbb R^{34}
 \xrightarrow{\ 34\to32\ }\mathrm{GELU}
 \xrightarrow{\ 32\to2\ }(J^{\rm R}_{\bm k i},J^{\rm I}_{\bm k i}),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 J_{\bm k i}=J^{\rm R}_{\bm k i}+iJ^{\rm I}_{\bm k i},\qquad
 Q_{\bm k i}=J_{\bm k i}\varphi_{\bm k i}.
 \label{eq:strictonce}
\end{equation}
The direct complex output is not converted to $1+\alpha\Delta J$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:strictonce} is implemented once: $J$ is not included
inside $\varphi$ and then multiplied again. A unit test that sets
$J_{\bm k i}=1/2$ verifies
$\det Q=2^{-N_e}\det\varphi$ to numerical precision.

For the Luo--Fu full-$M$ state,
\begin{align}
 \Phi_{\gamma i}(X)
 &=\sum_{\bm k}M_{\gamma\bm k}Q_{\bm k i}(X),\\
 \Psi_M(X)&=\det_{\gamma i}\Phi_{\gamma i}(X),
 \qquad M\in\mathbb C^{N_e\times9}.
 \label{eq:fullM}
\end{align}
All graph, $W$, $J$, and complex $M$ parameters are trainable. Only one final
generalized Slater determinant is used. Because $M$ is trainable and dense,
the Cauchy--Binet expansion of $\det(MQ)$ generally contains several
total-momentum sectors.

Version 4 starts from the same single dense $M$ but restores exact total
momentum by averaging the complete determinant over the nine translations
$T$ of the $3\times3$ torus. Let
$D_T=\mathrm{diag}[\exp(i\bm k\cdot\bm T)]$ act on the momentum rows of $Q$.
The new raw state is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Psi_\Gamma^{\rm v4}(X)=\frac1{9}\sum_T
 \det\!\left[M D_T Q(X)\right].}
 \label{eq:v4gamma}
\end{equation}
Only one $N_e\times9$ matrix $M$ is stored and optimized. The graph and $Q$
are evaluated once for $X$; the nine inexpensive contractions and
$N_e\times N_e$ determinants implement the group average. There is no list of
momentum combinations and no Monte Carlo sampling over such combinations.

Let $\mathcal C_\Gamma^{(N_e)}$ contain the $N_e$-momentum subsets with total
momentum $\Gamma$. Cauchy--Binet makes the many-body content explicit:
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_\Gamma^{\rm v4}(X)=
 \sum_{I\in\mathcal C_\Gamma^{(N_e)}}
 \det(M_{:,I})\det(Q_{I,:}(X)).
 \label{eq:v4-cauchy-binet}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $9^{-1}\sum_T\exp(i\bm K_I\cdot\bm T)$ is one for
$\bm K_I=\Gamma$ and zero otherwise. Equation~\eqref{eq:v4gamma} is therefore
an exact $\Gamma$-sector superposition rather than a one-hot determinant,
while its coefficients are the structured minors of a single-particle
matrix. For $N_e=3$ and 6, the implementation was verified numerically
against the explicit complete $\Gamma$-minor sum.

neither an individual displacement nor an individual generalized orbital is
forced to be a $C_3$ eigenobject, while the complete raw state remains in the
translation sector $\bm K_{\rm tot}=\Gamma$.

The physical continuum action includes the layer-dependent Bloch-gauge sewing.
With the code's active-rotation convention, define
\begin{equation}
 (\mathcal C^aD_\theta^{\rm v4})(X)
 =G_a(X)D_\theta^{\rm v4}(C_3^aX),
\end{equation}
where $G_0=1$ and $G_a$ is the accumulated product of the physical layer-gauge

$\omega=\exp(2\pi i/3)$, the complete-state projector is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \Psi_{\Gamma,m,\theta}^{\rm v4}(X)&=P_mD_\theta^{\rm v4}(X)\\
 &=\frac13\sum_{a=0}^{2}\omega^{-ma}G_a(X)\\[-2pt]
 &\quad\times D_\theta^{\rm v4}(C_3^aX),\qquad m=0,1,2 .
 \end{aligned}}
 \label{eq:outerc3}

\section{Hamiltonian and local-energy estimator}

The VMC objective uses the complete continuum Hamiltonian at
$\theta=3^\circ$, screening parameter $\epsilon=5$, and a $3\times3$ torus.
It is not the one-band or five-band projected ED Hamiltonian. The local energy
contains the kinetic term, layer-dependent moir\'e potential, spatially
varying interlayer tunnelling, and periodic Coulomb interaction,
\begin{equation}
 E_{\rm loc}(X)=\frac{(H\Psi)(X)}{\Psi(X)}
 =E_{\rm kin}+E_{\rm moir\acute e}+E_{\rm tun}+E_{\rm C}.
\end{equation}
The Coulomb sum uses reciprocal-shell cutoff 8, dielectric $\epsilon=5$, and
removes the $\bm q=0$ neutral-background component. Tunnelling is evaluated
by flipping one layer index and multiplying the exact wavefunction ratio.

Cartesian derivatives of the complete complex state use the three-point
central stencil
\begin{align}
 \partial_a\Psi/\Psi&\simeq
 \frac{\Psi(X+h\hat a)-\Psi(X-h\hat a)}{2h\Psi(X)},\\
 \partial_a^2\Psi/\Psi&\simeq
 \frac{\Psi(X+h\hat a)+\Psi(X-h\hat a)-2\Psi(X)}
 {h^2\Psi(X)} .
\end{align}
Here $h=1.5\times10^{-3}a_M$. Periodic wrapping is applied after every
coordinate displacement. A previous $h,h/2,h/4$ audit is not reused as a
validation of the new checkpoints; it must be repeated after convergence.

 V_{\rm M}(X)&=-2V\sum_{i,a}\cos(\bm g_a\cdot\bm r_i+\phi_{l_i}),\\

\section{MCMC and natural-gradient optimization}

There are 4,128 simultaneous walkers. Initial fractional coordinates are
uniform on the torus and layer indices are uniform in $\{0,1\}$. Each sweep
proposes, for every walker, one random-particle Gaussian displacement with
standard deviation 0.06 in fractional supercell coordinates, followed by one
random-particle layer flip. Both use the Metropolis ratio
$\min[1,|\Psi(X^\prime)/\Psi(X)|^2]$. The present jobs use 300 burn-in sweeps
and two sweeps per update. Acceptance for coordinate and layer moves is
recorded separately.

For real parameters $\theta_a$,
\begin{equation}
 O_a(X)=\partial_{\theta_a}\log\Psi_\theta(X),
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
 S_{ab}&=\operatorname{Re}\left[
 \langle O_a^*O_b\rangle-
 \langle O_a^*\rangle\langle O_b\rangle\right],\\
 F_a&=2\operatorname{Re}\left\langle
 (E_{\rm loc}-\langle E_{\rm loc}\rangle)O_a^*\right\rangle .
\end{align}
The update is
\begin{equation}
 (S+\lambda I)\Delta\theta=F,\qquad
 \theta\leftarrow\theta-\eta\Delta\theta,
 \label{eq:strictsr}
\end{equation}
with ordinary, unpreconditioned CG. JAX JVP/VJP operations apply the metric
matrix-free. The retained runs check at every Krylov step from iteration 20.
For the v4 outer runs at both fillings, checks occur at iterations
20,25,30,$\ldots$, and additionally whenever the recursive residual predicts
convergence or the iteration cap is reached. Acceptance always uses the
explicitly recomputed authoritative residual
\begin{equation}
 r_{\rm rel}^{\rm true}=\frac{\lVert F-(S+\lambda I)\Delta\theta\rVert_2}
 {\lVert F\rVert_2}
\end{equation}
with an additional Fisher-vector product. At a check, the solve stops when
$r_{\rm rel}^{\rm true}<0.02$ or at 60 CG iterations. At the cap, an update
is accepted only if $r_{\rm rel}^{\rm true}<0.05$ and
$F^{\mathsf T}\Delta\theta>0$. Otherwise the parameter update is rejected and
the following step uses the recovery values $\lambda=0.03$ and
$\eta=10^{-3}$. After two consecutive stable recovery solves, the protocol
returns to $\lambda=0.01$ and $\eta=2\times10^{-3}$. Every trace stores the
true residual, $F^{\mathsf T}\Delta\theta$, force and direction norms,
acceptances, and whether the update was applied. This is an adaptive truncated
natural gradient, not a claim that the $10^{-3}$ linear-system tolerance has
been reached.

\section{Controlled v4 single-$M$ projection experiments}

The historical full-$M$ jobs provide the unrestricted baseline. The two v4
$P_\Gamma[M]$ jobs optimize the exactly translation-projected single-$M$
state for 120 updates with 4,128 persistent walkers. The v4 outer runs at
both $\nu=1/3$ and $2/3$ use three same-seed parameter arrays, with seed
26090413 and 26090423 respectively. The arrays update independently from
step one, targeting
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=\frac13\sum_{m=0}^2
\frac{\langle\Psi_m(\theta_m)|H|\Psi_m(\theta_m)\rangle}
{\langle\Psi_m(\theta_m)|\Psi_m(\theta_m)\rangle}.
\end{equation}
During the first 20 updates, each sector contributes 1,376 persistent walkers
to a common 4,128-configuration pool. Every sector is evaluated on the full
pool. MBAR bridge equations estimate relative normalization constants and
sector importance weights~\cite{mbar}; energy, force and metric centering all
use these weights. This samples a probability mixture, not the coherent
superposition density. The equal factor $1/3$ is canceled between each sector's
force and metric, retaining the original per-sector damping convention.
The reported weight ESS measures concentration, not time-series
decorrelation. Finite-sample normalization and MCMC mixing remain
limitations. The exact last update-20 parameter state is retained,
without seed screening or best-checkpoint selection. Each branch receives a
copy of all 4,128 configurations and its own optimizer state, thermalizes for
300 proposal sweeps with a distinct RNG, and performs 100 private updates.
The final-ten training mean determines the displayed sector ordering.

\subsection{Direct plane-wave momentum occupation and bare-band weights}

The momentum occupation displayed in Figs.~\ref{fig:nbobservables-baseline}--
\ref{fig:nbobservables23} is not projected into a moir\'e band. Following the
momentum-resolved one-body density used by Li \emph{et al.}~\cite{limomentum},
it is evaluated directly in normalized layer plane waves at the 27 marked
first-BZ momenta. With $A$ the supercell area and
$\rho^{(1)}_l(\bm r,\bm r')=
\langle\psi_l^\dagger(\bm r)\psi_l(\bm r')\rangle$, define
\begin{align}
 n_{G=0}(\bm k)&=\sum_{l=1}^{2}\langle c^\dagger_{l\bm k}c_{l\bm k}\rangle\\
 &=\frac1A\sum_{l=1}^{2}\int_A d\bm r\,d\bm r'\,
 e^{i\bm k\cdot(\bm r-\bm r')}\rho^{(1)}_l(\bm r,\bm r').
\label{eq:total-nk}
\end{align}
The Monte Carlo estimator selects one electron and a uniform auxiliary
coordinate/layer and evaluates the complete one-particle replacement ratio.
The factor of two corrects the auxiliary-layer probability. Only the two
physical layers are summed: no $\bm k+\bm G$ image is folded into the displayed
value. Consequently the 27-point first-BZ sum is a finite plane-wave window
and is not constrained to equal $N_e$.

Bloch projection is used only for the separate band-weight diagnostic.  For
$a=(n,\bm k)$ and $b=(m,\bm q)$, its estimator is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 \rho_{ab}&=\langle c_b^\dagger c_a\rangle\\
 &=2N_e\,\mathbb E_{X\sim|\Psi|^2,i,x'}\!\left[
 (\phi_a^0(x_i))^*\phi_b^0(x')
 \left(\frac{\Psi(X_{i\to x'})}{\Psi(X)}\right)^*\right].
\end{split}
\label{eq:rdmratio}
\end{equation}
The factor two accounts for uniform auxiliary sampling of the two layers.  In
every ratio, replacing one particle triggers a complete new ansatz call: the
code rebuilds the graph and recomputes all particles' $\delta\bm r_j$,
$J_{\bm k j}$, and either the $M Q$ determinant or all nine translated
$M D_TQ$ determinants. The
projector is instead the fixed bare function
\begin{equation}
 \phi^0_{n\bm k}(\bm r,l)=\sum_{\bm G}u^l_{n\bm k\bm G}
 e^{i(\bm k+\bm G)\cdot\bm r};
\end{equation}
no backflow phase is multiplied into it.  Thus neural dressing appears once,
only through the complete wavefunction ratio.

For the nine-cell band-weight validation, all 4,128 final configurations and 16
auxiliary points per configuration were used to estimate the complete
$45\times45$ five-band-projected matrix. For the 27-cell figures, the same
complete-ratio construction evaluates the 135 diagonal elements directly;
these values are retained for the five band populations only, without
materializing a noisy $135\times135$ matrix. The band populations are
\begin{equation}
 W_n=\frac1{N_e}\sum_{\bm k}\rho_{n\bm k,n\bm k}.
\end{equation}

The estimator now caches $\Psi_m(X)$ once per configuration block and jointly

The focused bare-Slater regression sets $\delta\bm r=0$, $J=1$, fixes a

\subsection{Full structure factor, connected part, and density order}

Following the full-structure-factor definition of Luo, Zaklama and
Fu~\cite{luo}, the structure factor is evaluated directly as the Fourier
transform of the static density--density correlation function, not from a
Bloch-projected operator and not reconstructed from the 1-RDM:
\begin{align}
 S_{\rm full}(\bm q)&=\frac{\langle|\rho_{\bm q}|^2\rangle}{N_e},\\
 S_c(\bm q)&=\frac{\langle|\rho_{\bm q}|^2\rangle-
 |\langle\rho_{\bm q}\rangle|^2}{N_e},\\
 m_{\bm q}&=\frac{|\langle\rho_{\bm q}\rangle|}{N_e}.
\end{align}
Here $\rho_{\bm q}=\sum_i e^{i\bm q\cdot\bm r_i}$ includes every particle and
both layers. Each plotted $\bm q$ is the physical vector of the corresponding
dot in the hexagonal first Brillouin zone. There is no sum over $\bm q+\bm G$,
and the code does not replace a boundary point by a reciprocal-equivalent
representative, because the continuum density is not periodic under
$\bm q\mapsto\bm q+\bm G$. Five of the former 27 indices differed from their
plotted vectors by one primitive reciprocal vector; those indices are now
covered by a pointwise regression test. The ED comparison uses
$S_{\rm full}$ with the same normalization. The new
v4 values are evaluated independently in every sector, so differences among
the rows reflect different saved wavefunctions rather than duplicated plotting
data. The largest nonzero-$q$ density amplitudes are also reported to
distinguish a fluctuation peak in $S_{\rm full}$ from a nonzero static density
expectation.
The real-space panels in Figs.~\ref{fig:nbobservables13} and
\ref{fig:nbobservables23} include the intra-moir\'e-cell Bloch density and
should not be identified with a nonzero CDW order parameter by visual
contrast alone.

For the nine-cell validation, the largest nonzero-momentum values of $S_{\rm full}$ are
1.4818 for $P_\Gamma[M]$ and 1.4852, 1.1325 and 1.3018 for outer
$m=0,1,2$, compared with 1.4381 in five-band ED.  At $\nu=2/3$ the
corresponding values are 0.7677 and 0.7377, 0.6832 and 0.7013, compared with
0.6197 in ED.  The largest nonzero-$q$ density amplitudes are small:
$(0.01120,0.00902,0.01069,0.00719)$ for the $\nu=1/3$
$P_\Gamma,m=0,1,2$ states and
$(0.00475,0.00921,0.00590,0.00351)$ at $\nu=2/3$.  Thus the displayed
structure-factor maxima are predominantly fluctuation signals on these
finite training samples, rather than direct evidence for static density
order.

For 27 cells at $\nu=1/3$, the corrected first-BZ nonzero maxima for full $M$,
$P_\Gamma[M]$ and outer $m=0,1,2$ are respectively
$(1.1575,1.1722,1.5222,1.1937,1.2116)$, compared with 1.0571 in one-band
ED. In every case the maximum occurs at the conjugate $K,K'$ pair, for which
$S(K)=S(K')$ configuration by configuration. At $\nu=2/3$, the corresponding
QNS maxima are $(0.6976,0.7236,0.7492,0.7446,0.7254)$, again at $K,K'$, and
remain much larger than the one-band ED maximum 0.1968. Hence the present
120-update 27-cell states do not reproduce the weak one-band ED $\nu=2/3$
structure factor. Some difference is physically allowed because the QNS
uses the untruncated continuum Hilbert space, but the high rejected-update
counts and lack of an independent inference chain prevent attributing the
whole discrepancy to band mixing. Figures~\ref{fig:nbobservables-baseline}--
\ref{fig:nbobservables23} use one shared range covering all ED and QNS values,
so neither the $K$ peak nor the corrected boundary response is clipped.

The maximum nonzero density amplitudes $m_{\bm q}$ are 0.0920 and 0.0277 for
the momentum-unrestricted full-$M$ states at $\nu=1/3$ and $2/3$.
After exact translation projection they fall below 0.011 for every
$P_\Gamma[M]$ and outer state. The repeated intra-cell texture in the density
column is therefore mainly the moir\'e-scale one-body density, not evidence of
a large static translation-breaking order parameter. Together with the high
27-cell CG rejection counts, the $\nu=2/3$ structure-factor discrepancy is a
clear target for longer, better-conditioned and multi-seed optimization.

The corrected files obey $S(\bm q)=S(-\bm q)$ to roundoff because conjugate
density modes are evaluated on every configuration. We do not apply a
reciprocal-lattice wrap when auditing boundary points: such a wrap would test
$S(\bm q+\bm G)$ rather than the value of the dot actually drawn in the
continuum first BZ.

\section{Conclusion}

The implemented baseline follows the printed Luo--Fu Appendix-B structure:
fixed mean-field band-1 plane-wave coefficients, periodic node and edge
features, trainable random auxiliary features, two unshared message
iterations with the literal self-inclusive $\sum_jm_{ij}$, coordinate
backflow, direct complex $J_{\bm k i}$ applied exactly once, and one final
generalized Slater construction. Full $M$ mixes momenta at the single-particle
level and therefore generally mixes total-momentum sectors. Version 4 keeps
that single dense $M$ and applies the exact finite-translation projector to the
complete determinant. Cauchy--Binet proves that the result combines all and
only the total-$\Gamma$ minors with coefficients $\det M_{:,I}$, without
enumerating momentum combinations in the implementation.

The outer construction leaves that raw network unrestricted and projects the
complete $P_\Gamma[M]$ state with $P_m$. Character and projector-algebra
tests pass at double-precision tolerance, all three projected norms are
nonzero, and local energy,
Metropolis ratios and SR derivatives all use the projected state.  It adds no
trainable parameter. At nine cells the projected and unprojected models contain
the same 78,154 and 78,208 real parameters at $\nu=1/3$ and $2/3$; the
width-64 27-cell counterparts contain 571,374 and 571,860.

On the final-ten training tail at $\nu=1/3$, v4 $P_\Gamma[M]$ reaches
$-37.2576$ meV per particle and outer $P_0$ is the lowest projected branch at
$-37.3215$ meV. At $\nu=2/3$, the unrotated v4 state reaches $-52.7486$ meV;
$P_2$ is the lowest outer branch at $-52.9124$ meV per particle.
These are single-initialization training-chain diagnostics, so they do not
establish convergence or resolve seed sensitivity.

On 27 cells, full $M$ and $P_\Gamma[M]$ reach $-46.3697$ and $-46.6770$ meV
per particle at $\nu=1/3$, while outer $m=0$ is lowest at $-47.5342$ meV,
0.0612 meV above the one-band ED reference. At $\nu=2/3$, the respective
values are $-59.5784$ and $-59.8350$ meV, and outer $m=1$ is lowest at
$-60.4155$ meV. The $m=1,2$ tail means differ by only 0.0322 meV per particle,
but extensive residual-cap rejections prevent interpreting that proximity as
a converged topological multiplet. The observed per-state QNS training cost
is hours rather than the roughly 30-second cached one-band $\Gamma$ ED solve;
this comparison changes qualitatively for multiband ED, whose 27-cell Hilbert
space is not available here.

The 27-cell observables show that the projected $\nu=1/3,m=0$ state retains
zone-corner structure-factor enhancement. At $\nu=2/3$, however, all QNS
structure-factor maxima remain near 0.70--0.75, above the one-band ED
maximum 0.197, even though static nonzero-$q$ density amplitudes of all
translation-projected states stay below 0.011. Thus the current finite run
does not yet reproduce the ED liquid correlations. The complete-ratio
diagonal 1-RDM and direct two-body structure factors remain useful diagnostics,
but projected-trace noise, single-seed optimization and SR instability preclude
a natural-orbital or topological claim. The next controlled tests are longer
better-conditioned multi-seed optimization and sector-selection replication.
